## Appendix A: One-Page Quick Checklist

```yaml
Ad-hoc Collaboration Quick Checklist

▢ Chapter 1: Document Testability
▢ Can core claims be described in an "input → process → output" structure?
▢ Does the document contain clear boundary statements (what it cannot do)?
▢ Can proprietary terms be traced to independent sources, or do they form a closed self-referential loop?
▢ Is the document's tone primarily technical description, or primarily exclusionary/judgmental/closed?
▢ Is the core theory falsifiable — are there conditions under which it could be disproven?
▢ Are undelivered items honestly labeled as missing, or covered by rhetoric such as "the gap itself is data"?

▢ Chapter 2: Code–Claim Alignment
▢ Does each core claim have a corresponding implementation in the codebase (beyond mere naming)?
▢ Is the scale of the claims in the same order of magnitude as the codebase? (A nuclear plant claim requires nuclear plant code.)
▢ Can the core code be understood independently (without relying on documentation to explain it)?
▢ Can core functionality be run independently of the main project infrastructure?

▢ Chapter 3: Responsibility Attribution
▢ Who defines the standards? Does a single entity control both entry and exit?
▢ Is there any public record of responsibility attribution ("This is my part — if it is wrong, I will fix it")?
▢ What is the fulfillment rate of core milestones? When delayed, does anyone proactively explain?
▢ Has responsibility been shifted to you through tactics such as "authorization," "apology," or "praise"?

▢ Chapter 4: Participant Structure
▢ Is there a mutual-validation loop where "A confirms B, B confirms A"?
▢ Can the core mechanism be clearly explained to an external party by anyone?
▢ Do the capabilities of principal participants align with their outputs?
▢ Can your contributions stand independently and be cited outside the organization's framework?

▢ Chapter 5: Exit Cost
▢ Is your name bound to claims or products you have not independently validated?
▢ Does any theory or concept bear your surname that you have not validated?
▢ Does the organizer appear gentle on the surface but bypass review at critical junctures?
▢ Would your exit incur structural reputational or relational costs?

Final Determination:
If, in any single chapter, more than half of the check items are "No" or "Cannot answer" — that dimension is already showing red.
If two or more dimensions are simultaneously red — the evidence is sufficient; begin calculating your exit costs.
```

## Appendix B: External Anchors — An Operational Guide for Honest Collaborators

If you find yourself experiencing the above difficulties, the only effective form of self-protection is to establish **external anchors** — making your work independently visible to the world, without passing through the organization's framework.

### B.1 What Is an External Anchor?

An external anchor is a connection point between you and the publicly verifiable world: an independent repository, an independent benchmark, an independent publication channel. Their function is: **even if the organizational framework collapses, your work still exists, can still be cited, and still belongs to you.**

### B.2 Four Steps to Establish External Anchors

1. **Independent repository:** Publish your code, data, and validation records in a repository you control, using your own terminology, independent of the organization's naming system.

2. **Independent benchmark:** Establish benchmarks that do not rely on the organization's test suite, enabling external parties to reproduce your conclusions. Even if the organization claims "your data is invalid," your benchmarks remain independently verifiable.

3. **Independent naming:** Refuse to have your surname incorporated into any theoretical naming that you have not independently validated. If it has already been incorporated, publicly declare the naming frozen, with a note that "this theory has not yet received my independent validation."

4. **Independent publication:** Publish a summary of your work before or simultaneously with the organization's release, clearly marking your actual contribution boundaries — which parts were independently completed by you, which parts you assisted in reviewing, and which parts were led by others.

### B.3 Core Principle

> **When the organization's framework is the only channel for verification, the framework owner holds the exclusive interpretive power over your labor. External anchors are the only means to break this interpretive monopoly.**

## Appendix C: Legal and Platform Rules Reference

> This appendix is provided for reference and does not constitute legal advice. For specific legal matters, please consult qualified professionals.

### C.1 GitHub Reporting

If the other party continues to use your name or code on GitHub after you have explicitly withdrawn:

**Applicable scenarios:**
- The other party continues to list you as a contributor or collaborator in READMEs, documentation, or promotional materials
- The other party continues to use code you contributed after you have requested its removal or authorization withdrawal
- The other party continues to @mention you in Issues or PRs after you have explicitly declined further engagement

**Procedure:**
1. Visit the GitHub reporting page: https://github.com/contact/report-abuse
2. Select the appropriate report type (e.g., "Misuse of my personal information" or "Harassment")
3. Provide specific evidence: your public exit statement, GitHub links, screenshots
4. Submit and await GitHub review

**Note:** Reporting applies to violations of platform rules. GitHub does not mediate technical or legal disputes.

### C.2 DMCA Takedown

If the other party continues to use code you contributed without authorization after your exit, and you have explicitly withdrawn your authorization for its use:

**Applicable scenarios:**
- Your original contributed code remains in a repository, and the repository's usage has exceeded the scope you authorized
- You have clearly stated upon exit that you no longer authorize the use of your contributions
- The other party has failed to provide proper attribution or copyright notices as required by the open-source license

**Procedure:**
1. Identify the complete list of infringing content
2. Submit a formal notice via GitHub's DMCA form: https://github.com/contact/dmca
3. The notice must include: identification of the copyrighted work, location of the infringing material, your contact information, and a statement of good faith belief
4. GitHub will process the takedown request upon submission

**Note:** The DMCA process is a formal legal procedure. Submitting a false declaration carries legal liability. Before submitting, ensure your declaration is factually accurate, and be aware that the other party may file a counter-notice.

### C.3 Copyright Law Protection

Open-source contributions are protected by copyright law, regardless of the specific open-source license used. Your contributions are not "gifts to the project." If you have exited and requested unbinding, the other party's continued use of your code and data may constitute infringement.

**Relevant legal provisions for reference:**

**China:** Article 10 of the Copyright Law of the People's Republic of China establishes that copyright holders have the right to "authorize others to use their works." Article 11 states: "In the absence of proof to the contrary, the citizen, legal entity, or other organization whose name appears on a work is the author."

- You may demand that the other party cease using your code
- You may demand that the other party remove your attribution
- You may demand that the other party accurately state your actual role and contribution boundaries in derivative works

**International:** Open-source licenses (GPL, MIT, Apache, etc.) are legally recognized agreements. Violation of license terms may be found to constitute a violation of copyright law.
